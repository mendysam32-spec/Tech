<a id="nouns"></a>
## 1. NOUNS

### 1.1 Definition
A noun is a naming word. It names a person, place, animal, thing, idea, quality, feeling, or event.

### 1.2 Classification of Nouns

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Proper Noun** | Specific name of a person, place, day, month, organisation; always capitalised | Lagos, Kofi, WAEC, Nigeria, Monday, April, Mount Everest, Coca-Cola |
| **Common Noun** | General name shared by every member of a class | boy, city, school, book, teacher, car |
| **Concrete Noun** | Can be perceived by at least one of the five senses | table, water, music, perfume, heat, flower |
| **Abstract Noun** | Cannot be perceived by the senses; ideas, feelings, qualities, states | love, freedom, honesty, beauty, poverty, childhood, courage, wisdom |
| **Countable Noun** | Can be counted; has singular and plural forms | book/books, chair/chairs, student/students |
| **Uncountable (Mass) Noun** | Cannot be counted; no plural form; takes a singular verb | rice, water, information, advice, luggage, furniture, milk, bread |
| **Collective Noun** | Refers to a group regarded as one unit | team, crowd, committee, family, army, fleet, congregation, jury, flock |

### 1.3 Gender of Nouns

| Gender | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| Masculine | father, actor, lion, king, waiter, hero, prince |
| Feminine | mother, actress, lioness, queen, waitress, heroine, princess |
| Common | teacher, student, parent, cousin, doctor, clerk |
| Neuter | table, book, stone, computer, room, window |

### 1.4 Number (Plural Formation)

| Rule | Singular → Plural | Examples |
|------|-------------------|----------|
| Add **-s** | book → books | pen → pens, desk → desks |
| Add **-es** after -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -o | bus → buses | box → boxes, brush → brushes, mango → mangoes, potato → potatoes |
| Consonant + y → change y to **ies** | baby → babies | city → cities, country → countries |
| Vowel + y → add **s** | boy → boys | day → days, toy → toys |
| -f or -fe → change to **ves** | leaf → leaves | knife → knives, wife → wives |
| Irregular plurals | child → children | man → men, woman → women, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice, goose → geese, ox → oxen |
| Same form singular/plural | sheep → sheep | deer → deer, fish → fish, aircraft → aircraft |

### 1.5 Countable vs Uncountable: Determiner Choice

| Determiner | Use with | Examples |
|------------|----------|----------|
| many, few, a few, several, both | Countable only | many books, few students |
| much, little, a little, a great deal of | Uncountable only | much water, little rice |
| some, any, all, enough, a lot of, lots of | Both | some milk / some books |

### 1.6 Common WAEC Errors

- ❌ *The informations are useful.* → ✅ *The information is useful.* (uncountable)
- ❌ *I bought two furnitures.* → ✅ *I bought two pieces of furniture.*
- ❌ *She gave me an advice.* → ✅ *She gave me a piece of advice / some advice.*
- ❌ *He has many knowledges.* → ✅ *He has much knowledge.*

### 1.7 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify the type (common/proper, countable/uncountable, concrete/abstract) of an underlined noun.
- Supply correct plural forms in gap-filling.
- Use abstract nouns in compositions to show maturity of expression.
- Choose correct quantifiers (much/many, little/few) based on noun countability.

**Practice:** Write the plural of: child, criterion, phenomenon, analysis, curriculum, datum, appendix.

<a id="pronouns"></a>
## 2. PRONOUNS

### 2.1 Definition
A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

### 2.2 Types of Pronouns

| Type | Function | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Personal** | Replaces a person/thing; has subject and object forms | I, you, he, she, it, we, they / me, him, her, us, them |
| **Possessive** | Shows ownership (stands alone) | mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs |
| **Possessive Adjective** | Shows ownership (before noun) | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| **Reflexive** | Subject and object are the same person/thing | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves |
| **Emphatic** | Emphasises a noun/pronoun (same form as reflexive) | *I myself did it.* |
| **Demonstrative** | Points to specific persons/things | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | Used to ask questions | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| **Relative** | Introduces a relative clause | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | Refers to non-specific persons/things | anybody, somebody, everyone, nobody, all, some, any, none, each, either, neither, one |

### 2.3 Case of Personal Pronouns

**Subject (Nominative) case:** Used as subject of a verb.
- *She and I went to school.* (not *Her and me*)

**Object (Accusative) case:** Used as object of a verb or preposition.
- *The teacher praised him and me.* (not *him and I*)
- *Between you and me.* (not *between you and I*)

### 2.4 Possessive Pronoun vs Possessive Adjective
- *This is my book.* (my = possessive adjective + noun)
- *This book is mine.* (mine = possessive pronoun, stands alone)

### 2.5 Reflexive Pronoun Rules
- Use only when subject and object refer to the same person.
- ❌ *John and myself went.* → ✅ *John and I went.*
- ❌ *She gave the book to myself.* → ✅ *She gave the book to me.*

### 2.6 Indefinite Pronouns & Verb Agreement
- *Everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody, each, either, neither* → **singular verb**.
  - *Everyone has submitted his or her assignment.*
- *None* → singular or plural depending on meaning.
  - *None of the milk is spilt.* / *None of the students are present.*

### 2.7 WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct pronoun case in gap-filling.
- Identify errors in pronoun usage (e.g., *between you and I*).
- Use reflexive pronouns correctly.
- Agree verb with indefinite pronouns.

<a id="determiners"></a>
## 3. DETERMINERS

### 3.1 Definition
Determiners are words placed before a noun to specify which one, how many, or whose it is.

### 3.2 Types of Determiners

| Type | Examples | Usage Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|
| **Articles** | a, an, the | Definite/indefinite |
| **Demonstratives** | this, that, these, those | Near/far, singular/plural |
| **Possessives** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their | Ownership |
| **Quantifiers** | some, any, many, much, few, little, several, all, both, enough, no | Quantity |
| **Numbers** | one, two, first, second, next, last | Cardinal/ordinal |
| **Interrogatives** | which, what, whose | Questions |

### 3.3 Articles: Rules

| Article | Use | Examples |
|---------|-----|----------|
| **a** | Before consonant sound; singular countable; first mention | a book, a university (y-sound), a European |
| **an** | Before vowel sound; singular countable; first mention | an apple, an hour (silent h), an honest man |
| **the** | Before specific/unique nouns; second mention; superlatives | the sun, the Principal, the tallest boy, the book I bought |

**Omission of articles:** Before uncountable nouns in general sense (*Water is essential*), before plural nouns in general (*Books are important*), before names of languages (*English is easy*), before names of meals (*Breakfast is ready*), before school/church/prison/hospital when used for primary purpose (*He is in school*).

### 3.4 Demonstratives

| Near (Singular/Plural) | Far (Singular/Plural) |
|------------------------|-----------------------|
| this / these | that / those |
| *This pen is mine.* | *Those boys are late.* |

### 3.5 Quantifiers: Countable vs Uncountable

| Countable Only | Uncountable Only | Both |
|----------------|------------------|------|
| many, few, a few, several, both | much, little, a little, a great deal of | some, any, all, enough, a lot of, plenty of |

**Few vs A few:** *Few* = hardly any (negative). *A few* = some (positive).
**Little vs A little:** *Little* = hardly any (negative). *A little* = some (positive).

### 3.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose the correct determiner in gap-filling.
- Distinguish *few/a few*, *little/a little*.
- Use articles correctly in essays.
- Identify zero article contexts.

**Practice:** Fill the gaps: ___ water is essential for life. (The / No article). I have ___ friends in Lagos. (few / a few).

<a id="verbs"></a>
## 4. VERBS

### 4.1 Definition
A verb is a doing or action word. It expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being.

### 4.2 Types of Verbs

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Main (Action) Verb** | Expresses physical or mental action | run, write, think, eat, dance, sing |
| **Linking (Copular) Verb** | Connects subject to subject complement | be, seem, become, appear, look, feel, smell, taste |
| **Auxiliary (Helping) Verb** | Helps main verb form tense/voice/question | be, have, do |
| **Modal Auxiliary** | Expresses ability, permission, obligation, possibility | can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to |
| **Semi-modal** | Behaves like modals | need, dare, used to |
| **Transitive Verb** | Takes a direct object | She *killed* a snake. |
| **Intransitive Verb** | Does not take a direct object | The baby *slept*. |

### 4.3 Principal Parts of Verbs

| Base Form | Past Simple | Past Participle | -ing Form |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| walk | walked | walked | walking |
| go | went | gone | going |
| write | wrote | written | writing |
| see | saw | seen | seeing |
| cut | cut | cut | cutting |
| put | put | put | putting |
| begin | began | begun | beginning |

### 4.4 Regular vs Irregular Verbs
- **Regular:** Add -ed for past and past participle (play → played → played).
- **Irregular:** Change form internally (go → went → gone; see → saw → seen).

### 4.5 Modal Verbs: Uses

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| can | ability, permission | She can swim. |
| could | past ability, polite request | Could you help me? |
| may | permission, possibility | It may rain. |
| might | weaker possibility | He might come. |
| shall | future (first person), suggestion | Shall we go? |
| should | advice, obligation | You should study. |
| will | future, promise | I will call you. |
| would | past of will, polite request | Would you like tea? |
| must | strong obligation, necessity | You must stop. |
| ought to | moral obligation | You ought to respect elders. |

**Rule:** Modal + base verb (no -s, no -ed, no -ing). ❌ *He can to swim.* ✅ *He can swim.*

### 4.6 Transitive vs Intransitive
- Transitive: *The hunter killed a lion.* (lion = direct object)
- Intransitive: *The lion roared.* (no object)
- Some verbs can be both: *She sings.* / *She sings a song.*

**Note:** Intransitive verbs cannot be made passive.

### 4.7 Phrasal Verbs (WAEC Oral/Structure)
Verb + adverb/preposition with idiomatic meaning.
- *give up* = surrender/stop
- *look after* = take care of
- *put off* = postpone
- *take off* = remove / (plane) depart
- *run out of* = exhaust supply

### 4.8 WAEC Exam Focus
- Supply correct past tense/past participle.
- Identify transitive/intransitive verbs.
- Use modals correctly in gap-filling.
- Recognise phrasal verbs in comprehension.

**Practice:** Give the past participle of: steal, break, speak, drive, lie (recline), lay (place).

<a id="adverbs"></a>
## 5. ADVERBS

### 5.1 Definition
An adverb modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It answers: how? when? where? how often? to what extent?

### 5.2 Types of Adverbs

| Type | Examples | Question Answered | Example Sentence |
|------|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Manner** | slowly, quickly, carefully, well, badly, sweetly | How? | *She sings beautifully.* |
| **Time** | now, yesterday, soon, later, today, then | When? | *He will arrive tomorrow.* |
| **Place** | here, there, everywhere, outside, below, upstairs | Where? | *The children are outside.* |
| **Frequency** | always, never, often, rarely, sometimes, usually, seldom | How often? | *He always comes early.* |
| **Degree** | very, quite, extremely, almost, too, rather | To what extent? | *She is very intelligent.* |
| **Interrogative** | when, where, why, how | Questions | *When did you come?* |
| **Relative** | where, when, why | Introduces clauses | *This is the house where I live.* |

### 5.3 Formation of Adverbs
- Most: adjective + **-ly**: quick → quickly, careful → carefully, happy → happily.
- Adjective ending in -y: change y to i + ly: happy → happily, easy → easily.
- Adjective ending in -le: change -le to -ly: gentle → gently, terrible → terribly.
- **Exceptions (same form):** fast → fast, hard → hard, late → late, early → early, daily → daily.
- **Irregular:** good → well.

### 5.4 Position of Adverbs

| Type | Position | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| Manner | After verb or object | *She speaks English fluently.* |
| Frequency | Before main verb; after be | *He always studies.* / *She is always punctual.* |
| Time/Place | Usually end of clause | *I will see you tomorrow.* / *Wait here.* |
| Degree | Before adjective/adverb | *It is extremely hot.* |

### 5.5 Adjective vs Adverb (Common WAEC Trap)
- ❌ *He is a quick runner.* (quick = adjective modifying runner — correct)
- ❌ *He runs quick.* → ✅ *He runs quickly.* (quickly = adverb modifying runs)
- ❌ *She looks beautifully.* → ✅ *She looks beautiful.* (linking verb takes adjective)

### 5.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify the word modified by an underlined adverb.
- Distinguish adjective vs adverb forms.
- Choose correct adverb form in gap-filling.

**Practice:** Change to adverb: careful, happy, terrible, good, fast.

<a id="the-adjective"></a>
## 6. THE ADJECTIVE

### 6.1 Definition
An adjective describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

### 6.2 Types of Adjectives

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Quality** | beautiful, tall, intelligent, wicked, honest, lazy |
| **Quantity** | some, much, little, enough, all, no, any |
| **Number** | one, two, first, second, many, few, several |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| **Interrogative** | which, what, whose |
| **Proper** | Nigerian, English, African, Shakespearean, Parisian |
| **Emphasising** | own, very, same |

### 6.3 Degrees of Comparison

| Degree | Formation | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Positive** | Base form | tall, beautiful, good |
| **Comparative** | -er / more + adjective | taller, more beautiful, better |
| **Superlative** | -est / most + adjective | tallest, most beautiful, best |

**Rules:**
- One syllable: add -er/-est (tall → taller → tallest)
- Two syllables ending in -y: change y to i + -er/-est (happy → happier → happiest)
- Two or more syllables: more/most (beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful)
- **Irregular:** good → better → best; bad → worse → worst; little → less → least; much/many → more → most; far → farther/further → farthest/furthest.

### 6.4 Comparison Structures
- *as … as* (positive in comparisons of equality): *John is as tall as Mary.*
- *than* after comparative: *She is taller than her sister.*
- *the … of all / in* after superlative: *He is the tallest of all.*

### 6.5 Common Errors
- ❌ *more taller* → ✅ *taller* (double comparative)
- ❌ *the most tallest* → ✅ *the tallest* (double superlative)
- ❌ *He is taller than me.* (acceptable informally; formally: *taller than I*)

### 6.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Supply correct degree of comparison.
- Identify adjectives and the nouns they modify.
- Avoid double comparatives/superlatives.

**Practice:** Compare: good, bad, little, much, far, beautiful.

<a id="simple-sentences"></a>
## 7. SIMPLE SENTENCES

### 7.1 Definition
A simple sentence contains **one independent clause** — one subject and one predicate — expressing a complete thought.

### 7.2 Structure
**Subject + Verb + (Object / Complement / Adverbial)**

### 7.3 Examples
- *The bell rang.*
- *Fatima reads novels every night.*
- *The teachers marked the scripts.*
- *My father bought me a car.* (Subject: My father; Verb: bought; Indirect Object: me; Direct Object: a car)

### 7.4 Compound Elements within Simple Sentences
A sentence can have compound subjects/predicates and still be simple:
- *John and Mary are friends.* (compound subject)
- *She cooked and ate.* (compound predicate)
- *The boys and girls sang and danced.* (both compound)

### 7.5 WAEC Exam Focus
- Recognise simple sentences among options.
- Use simple sentences for clarity and emphasis in compositions.
- Do not confuse a simple sentence with a compound subject/predicate with a compound sentence.

<a id="tenses-forms"></a>
## 8. TENSES FORMS

Tense indicates the time of an action or state. English has **three times** (Present, Past, Future) × **four aspects** (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous) = **12 tenses**.

### 8.1 Present Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Main Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Present Simple | V1 (or V1 + s/es) | Habit, permanent fact, universal truth, timetable | *The sun rises in the east.* / *She goes to school daily.* |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + V-ing | Action happening now; temporary situation | *She is writing a letter.* |
| Present Perfect | has/have + V3 | Past action with present result; indefinite past | *I have finished my work.* |
| Present Perfect Continuous | has/have + been + V-ing | Action started in past, still continuing, emphasis on duration | *He has been sleeping since 6 p.m.* |

**Signal words:** always, usually, often, never, every day (Simple); now, at the moment, currently (Continuous); already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for (Perfect); since, for, all morning (Perfect Continuous).

### 8.2 Past Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Main Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Past Simple | V2 | Completed action at a definite past time | *She travelled yesterday.* |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | Action in progress at a past time; interrupted action | *They were playing when I arrived.* |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | Action completed before another past action | *The train had left before I came.* |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had + been + V-ing | Duration of an action up to a past point | *We had been waiting for two hours.* |

**Signal words:** yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago (Simple); when, while, as (Continuous); before, after, by the time, already (Perfect); for, since (Perfect Continuous).

### 8.3 Future Tenses

| Tense | Structure | Main Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Future Simple | will/shall + V1 | Prediction, spontaneous decision, promise | *I will visit you tomorrow.* |
| Future Continuous | will be + V-ing | Action in progress at a future time | *This time tomorrow, I will be flying.* |
| Future Perfect | will have + V3 | Action completed before a future time | *By next week, she will have graduated.* |
| Future Perfect Continuous | will have been + V-ing | Duration up to a future point | *By 2026, I will have been teaching for ten years.* |

**Signal words:** tomorrow, next week, soon, in the future (Simple/Continuous); by, by the time (Perfect); for, since (Perfect Continuous).

### 8.4 Sequence of Tenses
When the main verb is in the past tense, the subordinate verb usually shifts to a past form.
- *Direct:* She says, "I am happy."
- *Reported:* She said that she **was** happy.

### 8.5 Common Errors
- ❌ *She don't like rice.* → ✅ *She doesn't like rice.* (Present Simple, 3rd person singular + s)
- ❌ *I have seen him yesterday.* → ✅ *I saw him yesterday.* (definite past time = Past Simple, not Present Perfect)
- ❌ *He is knowing the answer.* → ✅ *He knows the answer.* (stative verbs: know, believe, love, hate, want, need — not used in continuous)

### 8.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct tense in gap-filling and sentence completion.
- Apply sequence of tenses in reported speech.
- Use Past Simple for narrative essays; Present Simple for factual/expository essays.
- Identify stative verbs not used in continuous tenses.

**Practice:** Complete: By the time the teacher arrived, the students ___ (already / start) the test.

<a id="active-and-passive-voice"></a>
## 9. ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

### 9.1 Active Voice
The subject performs the action.
- *The farmer planted maize.*

### 9.2 Passive Voice
The subject receives the action. Focus is on the receiver/action, not the doer.
- *Maize was planted by the farmer.*

### 9.3 Passive Structure
**Object + appropriate form of be + past participle + (by + doer)**

### 9.4 Conversion Table

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Present Simple | She writes letters. | Letters are written by her. |
| Present Continuous | She is writing a letter. | A letter is being written by her. |
| Present Perfect | She has written letters. | Letters have been written by her. |
| Past Simple | She wrote letters. | Letters were written by her. |
| Past Continuous | She was writing a letter. | A letter was being written by her. |
| Past Perfect | She had written letters. | Letters had been written by her. |
| Future Simple | She will write letters. | Letters will be written by her. |
| Modal (must/can) | She must write letters. | Letters must be written by her. |

### 9.5 When to Use Passive
- Doer is unknown: *My purse was stolen.*
- Doer is unimportant: *The road is being repaired.*
- To sound formal/objective: *It is believed that hard work pays.*
- To emphasise the receiver: *WAEC was established in 1952.*

### 9.6 Limitations
- Intransitive verbs (sleep, die, arrive, happen, go) cannot be passivised because they have no object.
- ❌ *He was died.* ✅ *He died.*

### 9.7 WAEC Exam Focus
- Convert active to passive and vice versa.
- Identify the agent (doer) in passive sentences.
- Change questions and commands to passive:
  - *Did you lock the door?* → *Was the door locked by you?*
  - *Open the window.* → *Let the window be opened.*

<a id="subject-verb-agreement"></a>
## 10. SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT

### 10.1 Definition
The verb must agree with its subject in number (singular/plural) and person.

### 10.2 Key Rules

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Singular subject → singular verb | *The boy **runs**.* |
| 2 | Plural subject → plural verb | *The boys **run**.* |
| 3 | Compound subject with *and* → plural | *James and John **are** here.* |
| 4 | *Each, everyone, everybody, anybody, someone, nobody, one* → singular | *Everyone **has** a book.* |
| 5 | *Either…or / Neither…nor* → verb agrees with nearer subject | *Neither the boys nor the girl **is** guilty.* |
| 6 | Collective noun → singular (as unit) or plural (as individuals) | *The team **is** strong.* / *The team **are** arguing among themselves.* |
| 7 | *None* → singular or plural | *None of the milk **is** spilt.* / *None **are** perfect.* |
| 8 | *Some, any, all, most, half* → depends on noun after *of* | *Some of the milk **is** spilt.* / *Some of the books **are** missing.* |
| 9 | Title, name, quotation → singular | *'The Merchant of Venice' **is** a play.* |
| 10 | Distances, sums of money, periods of time → singular | *Ten miles **is** a long way.* / *Five thousand naira **is** enough.* |
| 11 | Subject separated by phrase → verb agrees with real subject | *The box **of chocolates** is on the table.* (subject: box) |

### 10.3 Common Errors
- ❌ *One of the boys are absent.* → ✅ *One of the boys is absent.*
- ❌ *The news are good.* → ✅ *The news is good.* (uncountable)
- ❌ *The data is correct.* → ✅ *The data are correct.* (plural of datum; though often singular in informal use)
- ❌ *Neither of them have come.* → ✅ *Neither of them has come.*

### 10.4 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify and correct agreement errors.
- Watch for intervening phrases between subject and verb.
- Use singular verbs with indefinite pronouns.

<a id="phrases"></a>
## 11. PHRASES

### 11.1 Definition
A phrase is a group of words without a subject-verb combination, functioning as a single part of speech. It does not express a complete thought.

### 11.2 Types of Phrases

| Type | Head Word | Example | Function |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Noun Phrase** | Noun | *The intelligent girl* | Subject/object |
| **Verb Phrase** | Verb | *has been reading* | Predicate |
| **Adjective Phrase** | Adjective | *very beautiful* | Modifies noun |
| **Adverbial Phrase** | Adverb | *in the morning* | Modifies verb |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | Preposition | *under the table* | Adjective/adverb |
| **Infinitive Phrase** | to + verb | *to read novels* | Noun/adj/adv |
| **Gerund Phrase** | -ing form | *Swimming is fun* | Noun |
| **Participial Phrase** | present/past participle | *The man standing there* | Adjective |

### 11.3 Phrase vs Clause
- **Phrase:** No subject + verb. *In the morning.*
- **Clause:** Has subject + verb. *When I arrived in the morning.*

### 11.4 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify phrases and their functions.
- Distinguish phrase from clause.
- Expand phrases into clauses and vice versa.

<a id="clauses"></a>
## 12. CLAUSES

### 12.1 Definition
A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a finite verb.

### 12.2 Main (Independent) Clause
Expresses complete thought; stands alone.
- *The bell rang.*

### 12.3 Subordinate (Dependent) Clause
Cannot stand alone; begins with subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun.
- *because it was late* / *when the teacher arrived*

### 12.4 Types of Subordinate Clauses

| Type | Introduced by | Function | Example |
|------|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Noun Clause** | that, who, what, whether, if, wh- words | Subject/Object/Complement | *I know **that he is honest.*** |
| **Adjective (Relative) Clause** | who, whom, whose, which, that | Modifies a noun | *The boy **who won** is my friend.* |
| **Adverbial Clause** | because, although, if, when, since, while, unless | Modifies verb/adjective/adverb | *She left **because she was tired.*** |

### 12.5 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify main and subordinate clauses.
- Name the type of an underlined clause.
- Combine sentences using clauses.

<a id="compound-sentences"></a>
## 13. COMPOUND SENTENCES

### 13.1 Definition
A compound sentence contains **two or more independent (main) clauses** joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon.

### 13.2 Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)
For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

### 13.3 Examples
- *The rain stopped, and the sun came out.*
- *John is intelligent, but he is lazy.*
- *You must study hard, or you will fail.*
- *He was tired, yet he continued working.*

### 13.4 Punctuation
Use a comma before the coordinating conjunction when joining two independent clauses.
- *She cooked, and he washed the dishes.*

### 13.5 WAEC Exam Focus

- Join two simple sentences into one compound sentence correctly.
- Use correct punctuation.

because, although, though, since, when, while, if, unless, until, before, after, as, as soon as, so that, in order that, wherever, whenever

<a id="conditional-sentences"></a>
## 16. CONDITIONAL SENTENCES

Conditional sentences express a condition and its result. They consist of an **if-clause** (condition) and a **main clause** (result).

### 16.1 Type 1: Real/Possible Condition (Future)

| Structure | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| If + present simple, will + base verb | Real and possible situations | *If you study hard, you will pass WAEC.* |

### 16.2 Type 2: Unreal/Imaginary Condition (Present)

| Structure | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| If + past simple, would + base verb | Unlikely or imaginary situations | *If I were you, I would apologise.* |

**Note:** Use *were* for all persons in formal Type 2: *If he were rich…*

### 16.3 Type 3: Unreal Past Condition

| Structure | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| If + past perfect, would have + past participle | Impossible past situations (regret/criticism) | *If she had studied, she would have passed.* |

### 16.4 Mixed Conditionals
Combine Type 3 condition (past) with Type 2 result (present).
- *If he had saved money (past), he would be rich now (present).*

### 16.5 Unless (= if not)
- *Unless you work hard, you will fail.* = *If you do not work hard, you will fail.*

### 16.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Complete conditional sentences with correct verb forms.
- Identify the type of conditional sentence.
- Convert *unless* to *if* and vice versa.

**Practice:** Complete: If she ___ (arrive) on time, she ___ (meet) the principal.

<a id="used-to"></a>
## 17. USED TO

### 17.1 Used to + base verb (Past habit/state)
Expresses something that happened regularly in the past but no longer happens.
- *I used to walk to school.* (I don't now.)
- *There used to be a cinema here.* (There isn't now.)
- *She used to be fat.* (She isn't now.)

**Negative:** *didn't use to*  
**Question:** *Did you use to ...?*

### 17.2 Be/get used to + noun or -ing (Accustomed)
- *Be used to:* *I am used to the heat.* / *I am used to waking up early.*
- *Get used to:* *He will get used to the cold weather.*

### 17.3 WAEC Exam Focus
- Distinguish *used to* (past habit) from *be used to* (accustomed).

<a id="conjunctions"></a>
## 18. CONJUNCTIONS

### 18.1 Definition
A conjunction joins words, phrases, or clauses.

### 18.2 Types

| Type | Examples | Function | Example |
|------|----------|----------|---------|
| **Coordinating** | and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so | Joins equal parts | *James and John are brothers.* |
| **Subordinating** | because, although, if, when, since, while, unless, after, before | Joins main to subordinate clause | *I stayed because I was sick.* |
| **Correlative** | either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or | Joins pairs | *Not only is she intelligent, but she is also hardworking.* |

### 18.3 Correlative Conjunction Rules
- *Both A and B:* both elements must be same grammatical form.
  - ❌ *She is both intelligent and works hard.* → ✅ *She is both intelligent and hardworking.*
- *Not only A but also B:* verb agrees with the nearer subject.
  - *Not only the teacher but also the students **are** happy.*

### 18.4 WAEC Exam Focus

- Use correlative pairs correctly.

<a id="punctuations"></a>
## 20. PUNCTUATIONS

### 20.1 Definition
Punctuation marks are symbols that organise writing and show pauses, stops, and meaning.

### 20.2 Punctuation Marks Table

| Mark | Name | Use | Example |
|------|------|-----|---------|
| . | Full stop | Ends declarative/imperative sentence; abbreviations | *I go to school.* / *etc.* |
| , | Comma | Separates items, clauses, phrases; after introductory elements | *I bought rice, beans, and oil.* / *However, he came.* |
| ? | Question mark | Ends direct question | *Are you ready?* |
| ! | Exclamation mark | Strong emotion/command | *Stop! What a day!* |
| : | Colon | Introduces list, quote, explanation | *Bring: pen, ruler.* |
| ; | Semicolon | Joins closely related independent clauses; separates complex list items | *He tried; he failed.* |
| ' | Apostrophe | Possession; contraction | *John's book / don't* |
| " " | Quotation marks | Direct speech; titles of short works | *'Welcome,' she said.* |
| – | Dash | Extra info; emphasis | *The winner—John—smiled.* |
| ( ) | Parentheses | Extra/non-essential info | *Lagos (the former capital)* |
| - | Hyphen | Compound words; line breaks | *mother-in-law* |
| … | Ellipsis | Omission; trailing off | *He said… then left.* |

### 20.3 Apostrophe Rules
- **Possession singular:** *'s* → *the boy's book*
- **Possession plural ending in s:** *s'* → *the boys' books*
- **Possession plural not ending in s:** *'s* → *the children's toys*
- **Contraction:** *it's = it is / it has* vs *its = possessive*

### 20.4 Quotation Marks in Direct Speech
- *'I am tired,' she said.* (comma inside quotes before dialogue tag)
- *'I am tired,' she said. 'I need to rest.'*

### 20.5 WAEC Exam Focus
- Punctuate unpunctuated passages.
- Use quotation marks in dialogue for narrative essays.
- Use apostrophes correctly in possession vs contraction.

<a id="relative-clause"></a>
## 21. RELATIVE CLAUSE

### 21.1 Definition
A relative clause is an adjective clause that modifies a noun. It begins with a relative pronoun.

### 21.2 Relative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Refers to | Case/Use | Example |
|---------|-----------|----------|---------|
| who | people | subject | *The man who called* |
| whom | people | object | *The man whom I saw* |
| whose | possession | possessive | *The woman whose car was stolen* |
| which | animals/things | subject/object | *The book which I read* |
| that | people/things | subject/object | *The girl that won* |

### 21.3 Defining vs Non-defining Relative Clauses

**Defining (Restrictive):** Essential to identify the noun; no commas.
- *The student who cheated was punished.* (Which student? The one who cheated.)

**Non-defining (Non-restrictive):** Adds extra information; commas required.
- *Mr. Ade, who is our principal, retired today.* (Extra info about Mr. Ade.)

### 21.4 WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct relative pronoun.
- Punctuate non-defining clauses with commas.
- Combine two sentences using a relative clause.

<a id="direct-and-reported-speech"></a>
## 22. DIRECT AND REPORTED SPEECH

### 22.1 Direct Speech
The exact words spoken, enclosed in quotation marks.
- *Ada said, "I am tired."*

### 22.2 Reported (Indirect) Speech
The speaker's meaning is reported without exact words; no quotation marks.
- *Ada said that she was tired.*

### 22.3 Changes in Reported Speech

| Element | Direct → Reported |
|---------|-------------------|
| am/is | was |
| are | were |
| has/have | had |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | must / had to |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next/following day |
| yesterday | the day before/the previous day |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| come | go |
| this week | that week |

### 22.4 Pronoun Changes
- *I* → *he/she*  
- *my* → *his/her*  
- *you* → *him/her/them* (depending on context)

### 22.5 Reported Questions

**Yes/No questions:** *asked if/whether*
- *Direct:* "Are you ready?" → *Reported:* She asked if I was ready.

**Wh- questions:** *asked + wh-word* (word order changes to statement)
- *Direct:* "Where are you going?" → *Reported:* She asked where I was going.

**Commands/Requests:** *ordered/told/asked + to-infinitive*
- *Direct:* "Sit down," the teacher said. → *Reported:* The teacher told me to sit down.

### 22.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Convert direct to reported speech and vice versa.
- Apply backshifting of tenses.
- Change time and place expressions.

### 32.1 Definition
A contraction is a shortened form of a word or group of words, with omitted letters replaced by an apostrophe.

- **It's** = it is/it has; **Its** = possessive (no apostrophe)

<a id="grammar-usage"></a>
## 33. USING PRONOUNS, VERBS AND MODIFIERS CORRECTLY

### 33.1 Using Pronouns Correctly
- Use subject pronouns as subjects: *She and I are friends.* (not *Her and I*)
- Use object pronouns as objects: *The teacher praised John and me.* (not *John and I*)
- Use reflexive pronouns only when subject and object are the same: *He hurt himself.*

### 33.2 Using Verbs Correctly
- Maintain tense consistency in a passage.
- Use correct past participle after has/have/had: *has gone* (not *has went*).
- Use modals followed by base verb: *can go* (not *can to go*).
- Subject-verb agreement.

### 33.3 Using Modifiers Correctly
- Place modifiers near the word they modify.
  - ❌ *She almost drove all the way to Lagos.* (unclear)
  - ✅ *She drove almost all the way to Lagos.*
- Avoid dangling modifiers:
  - ❌ *Walking to school, the rain started.* (rain cannot walk)
  - ✅ *Walking to school, I saw the rain start.*
- Avoid misplaced modifiers:
  - ❌ *I only saw the principal.* (implies I did nothing else)
  - ✅ *I saw only the principal.*

## 34. TEST ON GRAMMAR

This is an integrated grammar section testing correct usage in context.

### Practice Questions (with answers)

**Q1.** Choose the correct option:  
*Neither of the candidates ___ qualified for the post.*  
A. are  B. is  C. were  D. have been

**Q2.** Choose the correct option:

*"I will come tomorrow," he said.*

**Answer:** will you.

**Answer:** A. money

<a id="comprehension"></a>
## 35. COMPREHENSION

### 35.1 Definition
Comprehension tests ability to read, understand, interpret, and answer questions based on an unseen passage.

### 35.2 Types of Questions

| Type | Description | How to Answer |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **Literal** | Answer directly from passage | Locate and copy/paraphrase |
| **Inferential** | Read between the lines | Base conclusion on evidence in passage |
| **Vocabulary** | Meaning of words/phrases in context | Give meaning as used in passage |
| **Grammatical** | Identify parts of speech, clauses, figures | Name the item and its function |
| **Summary** | Summarise main points | Continuous prose, own words, within word limit |
| **Title** | Suggest suitable title | Capture main idea, short and catchy |

### 35.3 How to Answer Comprehension Questions
1. Read the passage **twice** — first for general understanding, then for details.
2. Read each question carefully; underline key words.
3. Locate the relevant part of the passage.
4. Answer in your own words unless asked to quote.
5. Use complete sentences.
6. For vocabulary questions, give the meaning as used in the passage.
7. For inference, base your answer on evidence in the passage.
8. Do not copy whole paragraphs; be concise.

### 35.4 Sample Passage and Questions

**Passage:**
Every year, thousands of students sit for the West African Senior School Certificate Examination. This examination is not only a test of knowledge but also of discipline, time management, and endurance. Candidates who prepare adequately by studying past questions, attending tutorials, and managing their time wisely usually perform excellently. On the other hand, those who rely on examination malpractice often find themselves disgraced when results are released. It is therefore advisable for every candidate to cultivate good study habits and avoid shortcuts.

**Questions & Answers:**

1. **What does the WASSCE test besides knowledge?**  
   *Answer:* It tests discipline, time management, and endurance.

2. **What do candidates who prepare adequately usually achieve?**  
   *Answer:* They usually perform excellently.

3. **What happens to candidates who rely on malpractice?**  
   *Answer:* They are often disgraced when results are released.

4. **Give the meaning of the word "adequately" as used in the passage.**  
   *Answer:* Enough / sufficiently / properly.

5. **Suggest a suitable title for the passage.**  
   *Answer:* Preparing for WASSCE / The Importance of Good Study Habits.

### 35.5 WAEC Exam Focus
- Paper 2 Section A: Comprehension passage + questions + summary.
- Manage time: about 40 minutes for comprehension.

## 36. COMPOSITION

### 36.1 Definition
Composition tests ability to write organized, coherent, and grammatically correct essays on given topics.

### 36.2 Types of WAEC Essays

| Type | Purpose | Features | Tense |
|------|---------|----------|-------|
| **Narrative** | Tell a story | Plot, characters, dialogue, setting, climax | Past |
| **Descriptive** | Describe person/place/thing | Sensory details, adjectives, vivid imagery | Present |
| **Argumentative** | Convince reader | Thesis, supporting points, counter-argument, conclusion | Present |
| **Expository** | Explain/inform | Facts, examples, definitions, clear organisation | Present |
| **Formal Letter** | Official communication | Address, date, salutation, body, closing, signature | Present |
| **Informal Letter** | Personal communication | Friendly tone, address, date, salutation | Past/Present |
| **Article** | Publication | Title, introduction, body, conclusion | Present |
| **Speech** | Public address | Salutation, introduction, body, conclusion, vote of thanks | Present |
| **Report** | Official account | Title, terms of reference, procedure, findings, conclusion | Past |
| **Debate** | Support/oppose motion | Salutation, definition of terms, arguments, conclusion | Present |

### 36.3 Essay Marking Criteria (WAEC)

| Criterion | Marks | What is assessed |
|-----------|-------|------------------|
| Content | 10 | Relevance, development of ideas, maturity of thought |
| Organisation | 10 | Paragraphing, coherence, logical order, introduction/conclusion |
| Expression | 10 | Vocabulary, sentence variety, register, tone |
| Mechanical Accuracy | 10 | Grammar, punctuation, spelling |
| **Total** | **40** | |

### 36.4 Essay Structure

**Introduction:** Hook, background, thesis statement (for argumentative/expository).  
**Body Paragraphs:** One main idea per paragraph; topic sentence; supporting details; examples; concluding sentence.  
**Conclusion:** Restate main points; final thought/recommendation; no new ideas.

### 36.5 Tips for High Scores
- Write 450–600 words (about 2–3 pages).
- Plan before writing (spend 5–10 minutes outlining).
- Use clear paragraphs.
- Vary sentence length and structure.
- Use appropriate register (formal for letters/articles, informal for letters to friends).
- Proofread for spelling, punctuation, and grammar errors.
- Use idioms and figurative language sparingly and appropriately.

### 36.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Paper 2 Section B: Choose one essay question from several options.
- Spend about 50 minutes on the essay.

## 38. ORAL ENGLISH (PAPER 1)

### 38.1 Vowels and Consonants
- **Pure vowels (monophthongs):** /i:/, /ɪ/, /e/, /æ/, /ɑ:/, /ɒ/, /ɔ:/, /ʊ/, /u:/, /ʌ/, /ɜ:/, /ə/
- **Diphthongs:** /eɪ/, /aɪ/, /ɔɪ/, /aʊ/, /əʊ/, /ɪə/, /eə/, /ʊə/
- **Consonants:** /p, b, t, d, k, g, f, v, θ, ð, s, z, ʃ, ʒ, h, tʃ, dʒ, m, n, ŋ, l, r, w, j/

### 38.2 Word Stress
- Stress the correct syllable: *reCORD* (noun: REcord), *susPEND*, *PUBlic*, *TEACHer*.
- Stress changes meaning: *CONvict* (noun) vs *conVICT* (verb).

### 38.3 Sentence Stress and Intonation
- **Rising intonation:** Yes/no questions (*Are you coming?*)
- **Falling intonation:** Statements, commands, wh-questions (*What is your name?*)

### 38.4 Rhyme
Words ending with the same sound: *cat/bat, light/night, go/know.*

### 38.5 Homophones in Oral English
Same pronunciation, different spelling/meaning: *flour/flower, hear/here.*

### 38.6 WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify vowel/consonant sounds.
- Identify stressed syllables.
- Identify intonation patterns.
- Identify rhyming words.
