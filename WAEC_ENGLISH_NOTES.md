<a id="nouns"></a>
## 1. NOUNS

### Definition
A noun is a naming word. It names a person, place, animal, thing, idea, quality, or event.

### Types of Nouns

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Proper Noun** | Specific name of a person, place, or organisation; always capitalised | Lagos, Kofi, WAEC, Nigeria, Mount Kilimanjaro |
| **Common Noun** | General name for a class of persons, places, or things | boy, city, school, book, teacher |
| **Concrete Noun** | Can be perceived by the five senses | table, water, music, perfume, heat |
| **Abstract Noun** | Cannot be perceived by the senses; ideas, feelings, qualities | love, freedom, honesty, beauty, poverty, childhood |
| **Countable Noun** | Can be counted; has singular and plural forms | book/books, chair/chairs, student/students |
| **Uncountable Noun** | Cannot be counted; no plural form; takes singular verb | rice, water, information, advice, luggage, furniture |
| **Collective Noun** | Refers to a group as one unit | team, crowd, committee, family, army, fleet, congregation |

### Gender of Nouns
- Masculine: father, actor, lion, king
- Feminine: mother, actress, lioness, queen
- Common: teacher, student, parent, cousin
- Neuter: table, book, stone, computer

### Number of Nouns (Plural Formation)
Most nouns add **-s**: book → books  
Nouns ending in -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x, -o add **-es**: bus → buses, box → boxes, mango → mangoes  
Nouns ending in consonant + y change y to ies: baby → babies, city → cities  
Irregular plurals: child → children, man → men, woman → women, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice, goose → geese, sheep → sheep, deer → deer

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify the type/number/gender of underlined nouns in objective questions.
- Use abstract nouns in composition to show maturity of expression.
- Distinguish countable and uncountable nouns when choosing determiners (much/many, little/few).

<a id="pronouns"></a>
## 2. PRONOUNS

### Definition
A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

### Types of Pronouns

| Type | Function | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Personal** | Replaces a person or thing | I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them |
| **Possessive** | Shows ownership | mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs |
| **Reflexive** | Refers back to the subject | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| **Demonstrative** | Points to specific things | this, that, these, those |
| **Interrogative** | Used to ask questions | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| **Relative** | Introduces a relative clause | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | Refers to non-specific persons/things | anybody, somebody, everyone, nobody, all, some, any, none, each, either, neither |

### Case of Personal Pronouns
- **Subject (Nominative):** I, he, she, we, they — used as subject of verb
  - *She and I went to school.*
- **Object (Accusative):** me, him, her, us, them — used as object of verb/preposition
  - *The teacher praised him and me.*

### Common WAEC Errors
- ❌ *Between you and I* → ✅ *Between you and me* (me is object of preposition)
- ❌ *John and myself went* → ✅ *John and I went* (myself cannot be subject)

<a id="determiners"></a>
## 3. DETERMINERS

### Definition
Determiners are words placed before a noun to show which one, how many, or whose it is.

### Types

| Type | Examples | Usage |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Articles** | a, an, the | Definite (the) / Indefinite (a, an) |
| **Demonstratives** | this, that, these, those | Pointing out |
| **Possessives** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their | Ownership |
| **Quantifiers** | some, any, many, much, few, little, several, all, both, enough | Quantity |
| **Numbers** | one, two, first, second | Counting/order |
| **Interrogatives** | which, what, whose | Questions |

### Rules for Articles
- **a** before consonant sound: a book, a university (y-sound)
- **an** before vowel sound: an apple, an hour (silent h)
- **the** before specific/unique nouns: the sun, the Principal, the book I bought

### Quantifiers: Countable vs Uncountable
- Countable: many, few, a few, several, both
- Uncountable: much, little, a little, a great deal of
- Both: some, any, all, enough, a lot of, lots of

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct determiner in gap-filling objective questions.
- Use *few/little* (negative: hardly any) vs *a few/a little* (positive: some).

<a id="verbs"></a>
## 4. VERBS

### Definition
A verb is a doing word or action word. It expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being.

### Types of Verbs

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Action (Main) Verb** | Expresses physical or mental action | run, write, think, eat, dance |
| **Linking Verb** | Connects subject to complement | be, seem, become, appear, look, feel |
| **Auxiliary (Helping) Verb** | Helps main verb form tense/voice/question | be, have, do, can, will, shall, may, must |
| **Modal Verb** | Expresses ability, permission, obligation, possibility | can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to |
| **Transitive Verb** | Takes a direct object | She *killed* a snake. |
| **Intransitive Verb** | No direct object | The baby *slept*. |

### Verb Forms (Principal Parts)
| Base | Past Simple | Past Participle | -ing form |
|------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| walk | walked | walked | walking |
| go | went | gone | going |
| write | wrote | written | writing |
| see | saw | seen | seeing |
| cut | cut | cut | cutting |

### Regular vs Irregular Verbs
- **Regular:** add -ed for past and past participle (play → played)
- **Irregular:** change form (go → went → gone)

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify transitive/intransitive verbs.
- Supply correct past tense/past participle in gap-filling.
- Use modals correctly (must = obligation, can = ability, may = permission).

<a id="adverbs"></a>
## 5. ADVERBS

### Definition
An adverb modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It often answers: how? when? where? to what extent? how often?

### Types of Adverbs

| Type | Examples | Questions Answered |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| **Manner** | slowly, quickly, carefully, well, badly | How? |
| **Time** | now, yesterday, soon, later, today | When? |
| **Place** | here, there, everywhere, outside, below | Where? |
| **Frequency** | always, never, often, rarely, sometimes | How often? |
| **Degree** | very, quite, extremely, almost, too | To what extent? |

### Formation
Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to adjectives: quick → quickly, careful → carefully  
Exceptions: fast → fast, hard → hard, well (good → well), late → late/lately

### Position of Adverbs
- Adverbs of manner usually after the verb: *She sings beautifully.*
- Adverbs of frequency before main verb, after be: *He always comes early.* / *She is always punctual.*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Distinguish adjective vs adverb: *He is a quick runner* (adj) vs *He runs quickly* (adv).
- Identify the word modified by an underlined adverb.

<a id="the-adjective"></a>
## 6. THE ADJECTIVE

### Definition
An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

### Types of Adjectives

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Quality** | beautiful, tall, intelligent, wicked, honest |
| **Quantity** | some, much, little, enough, all, no |
| **Number** | one, two, first, second, many, few |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| **Interrogative** | which, what, whose |
| **Proper** | Nigerian, English, African, Shakespearean |

### Degrees of Comparison

| Degree | Form | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Positive** | Base form | tall, beautiful, good |
| **Comparative** | -er / more + adj | taller, more beautiful, better |
| **Superlative** | -est / most + adj | tallest, most beautiful, best |

**Rules:**
- One syllable: add -er/-est (tall → taller → tallest)
- Two+ syllables: more/most (beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful)
- Irregular: good → better → best; bad → worse → worst; little → less → least; far → farther/further → farthest/furthest

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Supply correct degree of comparison.
- Identify adjectives and the nouns they modify.
- Avoid double comparatives: ❌ *more taller* ✅ *taller*

<a id="simple-sentences"></a>
## 7. SIMPLE SENTENCES

### Definition
A simple sentence contains **one independent clause** — one subject and one predicate, expressing a complete thought.

### Structure
**Subject + Verb + (Object/Complement)**

### Examples
- *The bell rang.*
- *Fatima reads novels every night.*
- *The teachers marked the scripts.*

### Simple Sentence Variants
- Compound subject: *John and Mary are friends.*
- Compound predicate: *She cooked and ate.*
- Both compound: *The boys and girls sang and danced.*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Recognise simple sentences among options in objective questions.
- Use simple sentences for clarity and emphasis in composition.

<a id="tenses-forms"></a>
## 8. TENSES FORMS

Tense shows the time of an action. English has **three main times**: Present, Past, Future. Each has four aspects: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous = **12 tenses**.

### PRESENT TENSES

| Tense | Structure | Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|-----|---------|
| Present Simple | V1 / V1 + s | Habit, fact, truth | *The sun rises in the east.* |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + V-ing | Action happening now | *She is writing a letter.* |
| Present Perfect | has/have + V3 | Past action with present result | *I have finished my work.* |
| Present Perfect Cont. | has/have + been + V-ing | Action started past, still continuing | *He has been sleeping since 6 p.m.* |

### PAST TENSES

| Tense | Structure | Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|-----|---------|
| Past Simple | V2 | Completed past action | *She travelled yesterday.* |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | Action in progress in past | *They were playing when I arrived.* |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | Action completed before another past action | *The train had left before I came.* |
| Past Perfect Cont. | had + been + V-ing | Duration before a past event | *We had been waiting for two hours.* |

### FUTURE TENSES

| Tense | Structure | Use | Example |
|-------|-----------|-----|---------|
| Future Simple | will/shall + V1 | Prediction, decision, promise | *I will visit you tomorrow.* |
| Future Continuous | will be + V-ing | Action in progress in future | *This time tomorrow, I will be flying.* |
| Future Perfect | will have + V3 | Completed before future time | *By next week, she will have graduated.* |
| Future Perf. Cont. | will have been + V-ing | Duration up to future point | *By 2026, I will have been teaching for ten years.* |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct tense in gap-filling and sentence completion.
- Sequence of tenses in reported speech.
- Use past simple for WAEC narrative essays; use present for factual/expository essays.

<a id="active-and-passive-voice"></a>
## 9. ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

### Active Voice
Subject performs the action.
- *The farmer planted maize.*

### Passive Voice
Subject receives the action. Focus is on the action/receiver, not the doer.
- *Maize was planted by the farmer.*

### Conversion Table

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Present Simple | She writes letters. | Letters are written by her. |
| Past Simple | She wrote letters. | Letters were written by her. |
| Present Perfect | She has written letters. | Letters have been written by her. |
| Past Perfect | She had written letters. | Letters had been written by her. |
| Future Simple | She will write letters. | Letters will be written by her. |
| Modal | She must write letters. | Letters must be written by her. |

### Passive Structure
**Object + be + V3 + (by + doer)**

### When to Use Passive
- When the doer is unknown: *My purse was stolen.*
- When the doer is unimportant: *The road is being repaired.*
- To sound formal/objective: *It is believed that...*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Convert active to passive and vice versa.
- Note: Intransitive verbs (sleep, die, arrive) cannot be passivised.

<a id="subject-verb-agreement"></a>
## 10. SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT

### Definition
The verb must agree with its subject in number (singular/plural) and person.

### Key Rules

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Singular subject → singular verb | *The boy **runs**.* |
| Plural subject → plural verb | *The boys **run**.* |
| Compound subject with *and* → plural | *James and John **are** here.* |
| *Each, everyone, everybody, anybody, someone* → singular | *Everyone **has** a book.* |
| *Either ... or / Neither ... nor* → verb agrees with nearer subject | *Neither the boys nor the girl **is** guilty.* |
| Collective noun → singular (unit) / plural (members) | *The team **is** strong.* / *The team **are** arguing.* |
| *None* → singular or plural depending on meaning | *None of the students **was** present.* / *None **are** perfect.* |
| *Some, any, all, most* → depends on noun after | *Some of the milk **is** spilt.* / *Some of the books **are** missing.* |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify and correct errors in agreement in objective questions.
- Watch for intervening phrases: *The box of chocolates **is** on the table* (subject is box, not chocolates).

<a id="phrases"></a>
## 11. PHRASES

### Definition
A phrase is a group of words without a subject-verb combination, functioning as a single part of speech. It does not express a complete thought.

### Types of Phrases

| Type | Head Word | Example | Function |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Noun Phrase** | Noun | *The intelligent girl* | Subject/object |
| **Verb Phrase** | Verb | *has been reading* | Verb in clause |
| **Adjective Phrase** | Adjective | *very beautiful* | Modifies noun |
| **Adverbial Phrase** | Adverb | *in the morning* | Modifies verb |
| **Prepositional Phrase** | Preposition | *under the table* | Adjective/adverb |
| **Infinitive Phrase** | to + verb | *to read novels* | Noun/adj/adv |
| **Gerund Phrase** | -ing noun | *Swimming is fun* | Noun |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify phrases and their functions in sentences.
- Distinguish phrase from clause (phrase = no subject+verb).

<a id="clauses"></a>
## 12. CLAUSES

### Definition
A clause is a group of words containing a subject and a verb.

### Main (Independent) Clause
Expresses complete thought; can stand alone.
- *The bell rang.*

### Subordinate (Dependent) Clause
Cannot stand alone; begins with subordinating conjunction.
- *because it was late* / *when the teacher arrived*

### Types of Subordinate Clauses

| Type | Introduced by | Function | Example |
|------|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Noun Clause** | that, who, what, whether | Noun (subject/object) | *I know **that he is honest.*** |
| **Adjective (Relative) Clause** | who, whom, whose, which, that | Modifies noun | *The boy **who won** is my friend.* |
| **Adverbial Clause** | because, although, if, when, since | Modifies verb | *She left **because she was tired.*** |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify main and subordinate clauses.
- Name the type of underlined clause.

<a id="compound-sentences"></a>
## 13. COMPOUND SENTENCES

### Definition
A compound sentence contains **two or more independent (main) clauses** joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon.

### Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)
For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

### Examples
- *The rain stopped, and the sun came out.*
- *John is intelligent, but he is lazy.*
- *You must study hard, or you will fail.*

### Punctuation
Use a comma before the coordinating conjunction when joining two independent clauses.

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify compound sentences.
- Join simple sentences into compound sentences correctly.

<a id="complex-sentences"></a>
## 14. COMPLEX SENTENCES

### Definition
A complex sentence contains **one main clause and one or more subordinate clauses**.

### Examples
- *Although it rained, the match continued.* (Adverbial clause)
- *The girl who won the prize is my sister.* (Adjective/relative clause)
- *I believe that he is telling the truth.* (Noun clause)

### Common Subordinating Conjunctions
because, although, though, since, when, while, if, unless, until, before, after, as, as soon as, so that, in order that

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Distinguish complex from compound sentences.
- Identify main and subordinate clauses in complex sentences.

<a id="conditional-sentences"></a>
## 15. CONDITIONAL SENTENCES

Conditional sentences express a condition and its result. They have an **if-clause** (condition) and a **main clause** (result).

### Type 1: Real/Possible Condition (Present/Future)
**Structure:** If + present simple, will + base verb  
**Use:** Real and possible situations  
- *If you study hard, you will pass WAEC.*
- *If it rains, we will stay indoors.*

### Type 2: Unreal/Imaginary Condition (Present)
**Structure:** If + past simple, would + base verb  
**Use:** Unlikely or imaginary situations  
- *If I were you, I would apologise.*
- *If he had money, he would buy a car.*

### Type 3: Unreal Past Condition (Past)
**Structure:** If + past perfect, would have + past participle  
**Use:** Impossible past situations (regret/criticism)  
- *If she had studied, she would have passed.*
- *If they had left early, they would not have missed the bus.*

### Mixed Conditionals
Combine Type 2 and Type 3 when time references differ.
- *If he had saved money (past), he would be rich now (present).*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Complete conditional sentences with correct verb forms.
- Identify the type of conditional sentence.

<a id="used-to"></a>
## 16. USED TO

### 1. Used to + base verb (Past habit/state)
Expresses something that happened regularly in the past but no longer happens.
- *I used to walk to school.* (I don't now.)
- *There used to be a cinema here.* (There isn't now.)
- *She used to be fat.* (She isn't now.)

**Negative:** didn't use to  
**Question:** Did you use to ...?

### 2. Be used to + noun/-ing (Accustomed)
Expresses familiarity/accustomedness.
- *I am used to the heat.* / *I am used to waking up early.*

### 3. Get used to + noun/-ing (Become accustomed)
- *He will get used to the cold weather.*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Distinguish *used to* (past habit) from *be used to* (accustomed).
- Fill gaps with correct form.

<a id="conjunctions"></a>
## 17. CONJUNCTIONS

### Definition
A conjunction joins words, phrases, or clauses.

### Types

| Type | Examples | Function |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Coordinating** | and, but, or, nor, for, yet, so | Joins equal parts |
| **Subordinating** | because, although, if, when, since, while, unless | Joins main to subordinate clause |
| **Correlative** | either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also | Joins pairs |

### Examples
- *James and John are brothers.* (coordinates nouns)
- *I stayed because I was sick.* (subordinates adverbial clause)
- *Not only is she intelligent, but she is also hardworking.* (correlative)

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct conjunction in gap-filling.
- Use correlative pairs correctly: *both A and B*, *not only A but also B*.

<a id="prepositions"></a>
## 18. PREPOSITIONS

### Definition
A preposition shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and other words in a sentence (place, time, direction, manner, agent, instrument).

### Common Prepositions

**Place:** in, on, at, under, above, below, beside, between, among, behind, inside, outside  
**Time:** in, on, at, by, since, for, during, before, after, from...to  
**Direction:** to, into, onto, towards, through, across, along  
**Agent/Instrument:** by, with, from, of

### Important Rules
- *in* = enclosed space / months / years / seasons: in the room, in January, in 2024
- *on* = surface / days / dates: on the table, on Monday, on 25th December
- *at* = specific point / exact time: at the gate, at 6 p.m., at night
- *between* = two; *among* = more than two

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Supply correct preposition after verbs/adjectives: *interested in, afraid of, good at, married to, different from, consist of, depend on, suffer from, believe in.*

<a id="punctuations"></a>
## 19. PUNCTUATIONS

### Definition
Punctuation marks are symbols that organise writing and show pauses, stops, and meaning.

| Mark | Name | Use | Example |
|------|------|-----|---------|
| . | Full stop | Ends declarative/imperative sentence | *I go to school.* |
| , | Comma | Separates items, clauses, phrases | *I bought rice, beans, and oil.* |
| ? | Question mark | Ends direct question | *Are you ready?* |
| ! | Exclamation mark | Expresses strong emotion/command | *Stop! What a day!* |
| : | Colon | Introduces list, quote, explanation | *Bring: pen, ruler, eraser.* |
| ; | Semicolon | Joins closely related independent clauses | *He tried; he failed.* |
| ' | Apostrophe | Possession / contraction | *John's book / don't* |
| " " | Quotation marks | Direct speech / titles | *'Welcome,' she said.* |
| - | Hyphen | Joins compound words | *mother-in-law* |
| – | Dash | Adds emphasis or extra info | *The winner—John—smiled.* |
| ( ) | Parentheses | Extra/non-essential info | *Lagos (the former capital)* |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Punctuate unpunctuated passages in Paper 1 (objective) and essays.
- Use quotation marks correctly in dialogue for narrative essays.

<a id="relative-clause"></a>
## 20. RELATIVE CLAUSE

### Definition
A relative clause is a type of adjective clause that modifies a noun. It begins with a relative pronoun.

### Relative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Refers to | Use |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| who | people (subject) | *The man who called* |
| whom | people (object) | *The man whom I saw* |
| whose | possession | *The woman whose car was stolen* |
| which | animals/things | *The book which I read* |
| that | people/things | *The girl that won* |

### Defining vs Non-defining Relative Clauses

**Defining (Restrictive):** Essential to meaning; no commas.
- *The student who cheated was punished.* (Which student? The one who cheated.)

**Non-defining (Non-restrictive):** Extra information; commas required.
- *Mr. Ade, who is our principal, retired today.* (Extra info about Mr. Ade.)

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Choose correct relative pronoun.
- Punctuate non-defining clauses with commas.
- Combine sentences using relative clauses.

<a id="direct-and-reported-speech"></a>
## 21. DIRECT AND REPORTED SPEECH

### Direct Speech
The exact words spoken, enclosed in quotation marks.
- *Ada said, "I am tired."*

### Reported (Indirect) Speech
The speaker's meaning reported without exact words; no quotation marks.
- *Ada said that she was tired.*

### Changes in Reported Speech

| Direct | Reported |
|--------|----------|
| am/is → was | are → were |
| has/have → had | will → would |
| can → could | may → might |
| must → must/had to | this → that |
| today → that day | tomorrow → the next day |
| yesterday → the day before | ago → before |
| here → there | come → go |

### Pronoun Changes
- *I* → *he/she*  
- *my* → *his/her*  
- *you* → *him/her/them* (depending on context)

### Reported Questions
- Yes/No question: *asked if/whether*  
  *Direct:* "Are you ready?" → *Reported:* She asked if I was ready.
- Wh- question: *asked + wh-word*  
  *Direct:* "Where are you going?" → *Reported:* She asked where I was going.

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Convert direct to reported speech and vice versa.
- Watch backshifting of tenses and changes of time/place expressions.

<a id="filling-in-forms"></a>
## 22. FILLING IN FORMS

### Definition
This tests ability to complete official documents accurately — a functional writing skill examined in WAEC English.

### Common Forms
- School admission forms
- Examination registration forms (WAEC)
- Bank account opening forms
- Job application forms
- Passport/visa application forms
- Travel/booking forms

### Rules for Filling Forms
1. Read all instructions carefully before writing.
2. Use block/capital letters where required.
3. Write legibly.
4. Provide accurate information (name, age, date of birth, address, next of kin, religion, state of origin, local government, educational background).
5. Tick (✓) appropriate boxes where options are given.
6. Sign and date where required.
7. Do not leave compulsory fields blank; write *N/A* (Not Applicable) where appropriate.
8. Do not overwrite; cross out errors neatly and initial corrections.

### Typical Fields
- Surname, First name, Middle name
- Date of birth (DD/MM/YYYY)
- Place of birth

## 25. CORRECT AND INCORRECT USAGE OF ENGLISH LANGUAGE

This section tests ability to identify and correct grammatical errors — common in WAEC objective questions (ematics/structure).

### Common Errors and Corrections

| Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| He is more taller than me. | He is taller than me. | Double comparative |
| She don't like rice. | She doesn't like rice. | Subject-verb agreement |
| Between you and I | Between you and me | Object of preposition |
| The news are good. | The news is good. | Uncountable noun |
| He entered into the room. | He entered the room. | Enter takes no preposition |
| Discuss about the matter. | Discuss the matter. | Discuss is transitive |
| I am looking forward to meet you. | I am looking forward to meeting you. | to + gerund |
| One of the boys are absent. | One of the boys is absent. | One is singular |
| She is married with John. | She is married to John. | Correct preposition |
| He told me to wait here. | He told me to wait there. | Reported speech place change |
| The scissors is sharp. | The scissors are sharp. | Plural noun |
| Neither of them have come. | Neither of them has come. | Neither is singular |
| I prefer tea than coffee. | I prefer tea to coffee. | Prefer ... to |
| He is superior than me. | He is superior to me. | Superior/inferior take to |
| I have two sisters-in-laws. | I have two sisters-in-law. | Plural of compound noun |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify the sentence with no error / with an error.
- Rewrite sentences correctly.

<a id="grammar-test"></a>
## 32. USING PRONOUNS, VERBS AND MODIFIERS CORRECTLY — TEST ON GRAMMAR

This is an integrated grammar section testing correct usage in context.

### Using Pronouns Correctly
- Use subject pronouns as subjects: *She and I are friends.* (not *Her and I*)
- Use object pronouns as objects: *The teacher praised John and me.* (not *John and I*)
- Use reflexive pronouns only when subject and object are same: *He hurt himself.*

### Using Verbs Correctly
- Maintain tense consistency in a passage.
- Use correct past participle after has/have/had: *has gone* (not *has went*).
- Use modals followed by base verb: *can go* (not *can to go*).

### Using Modifiers Correctly
- Place modifiers near the word they modify to avoid ambiguity.
  - ❌ *She almost drove all the way to Lagos.* (unclear)
  - ✅ *She drove almost all the way to Lagos.*
- Avoid dangling modifiers:
  - ❌ *Walking to school, the rain started.* (rain cannot walk)
  - ✅ *Walking to school, I saw the rain start.*

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Identify sentences with correct/incorrect pronoun, verb, modifier use.
- Rewrite sentences correcting errors.

<a id="comprehension"></a>
## 33. COMPREHENSION

### Definition
Comprehension tests ability to read, understand, interpret, and answer questions based on an unseen passage.

### Types of Questions
1. **Literal:** Answer directly from the passage.
2. **Inferential:** Read between the lines; draw conclusions.
3. **Vocabulary:** Give meaning of words/phrases in context.
4. **Grammatical:** Identify parts of speech, clauses, figures of speech.
5. **Summary:** Summarise main points (usually in continuous prose).
6. **Title:** Suggest a suitable title for the passage.

### How to Answer Comprehension Questions
1. Read the passage **twice** — first for general understanding, then for details.
2. Read each question carefully; underline key words.
3. Locate the relevant part of the passage.
4. Answer in your own words unless asked to quote.
5. Use complete sentences.
6. For vocabulary questions, give the meaning as used in the passage (not dictionary meaning).
7. For inference, base your answer on evidence in the passage.
8. Do not copy whole paragraphs; be concise.

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Paper 2 (Essay) Section A: Comprehension passage + questions + summary.
- Manage time: about 40 minutes for comprehension.

## 34. COMPOSITION

### Definition
Composition tests ability to write organized, coherent, and grammatically correct essays on given topics.

### Types of WAEC Essays

| Type | Purpose | Features |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Narrative** | Tell a story | Past tense, sequence, dialogue, characters, plot |
| **Descriptive** | Describe a person/place/thing | Sensory details, adjectives, vivid imagery |
| **Argumentative** | Convince the reader | Thesis, supporting points, counter-argument, conclusion |
| **Expository** | Explain/inform | Facts, examples, definitions, clear organization |
| **Formal Letter** | Official communication | Address, date, salutation, body, closing, signature |
| **Informal Letter** | Personal communication | Friendly tone, address, date, salutation |
| **Article** | Publication | Title, introduction, body, conclusion |
| **Speech** | Public address | Salutation, introduction, body, conclusion, vote of thanks |
| **Report** | Official account | Title, terms of reference, procedure, findings, conclusion |
| **Debate** | Support/oppose motion | Salutation, definition of terms, arguments, conclusion |

### Essay Marking Criteria (WAEC)
1. **Content** (relevance, development of ideas) — 10 marks
2. **Organization** (paragraphing, coherence, logical order) — 10 marks
3. **Expression** (vocabulary, sentence variety, register) — 10 marks
4. **Mechanical Accuracy** (grammar, punctuation, spelling) — 10 marks  
**Total: 40 marks**

### Tips for High Scores
- Write 450–600 words (about 2–3 pages).
- Plan before writing (spend 5–10 minutes outlining).
- Use clear paragraphs: introduction, body (2–4 paragraphs), conclusion.
- Vary sentence length and structure.
- Use appropriate register (formal for letters/articles, informal for letters to friends).
- Proofread for spelling, punctuation, and grammar errors.

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Paper 2 Section B: Choose one essay question from several options.
- Spend about 50 minutes on the essay.

<a id="literature"></a>
## 35. LITERATURE

### Note
Literature-in-English is a separate WAEC subject but related to English Language through comprehension of prose, drama, and poetry.

### Elements of Literature

#### Prose (Novel/Short Story)
- **Plot:** Sequence of events (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution)
- **Setting:** Time and place of the story
- **Characters:** Protagonist, antagonist, minor characters
- **Theme:** Central message/idea
- **Point of View:** First person (I), third person (he/she), omniscient
- **Style/Technique:** Language, flashback, foreshadowing, symbolism

#### Drama (Play)
- **Dialogue:** Conversation between characters
- **Stage Directions:** Instructions in brackets
- **Act/Scene:** Divisions of the play
- **Tragedy/Comedy:** Serious vs humorous ending
- **Tragic Hero:** Noble character with a flaw (hamartia)
- **Conflict:** Struggle between opposing forces

#### Poetry
- **Stanza:** Group of lines (paragraph of poetry)
- **Verse/Line:** Single line of poetry
- **Rhyme Scheme:** Pattern of end rhymes (abab, aabb)
- **Rhythm/Meter:** Beat of the poem
- **Figures of Speech:** simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, alliteration, onomatopoeia, irony, euphemism, metaphor, synecdoche
- **Mood/Tone:** Feeling/attitude of the poet
- **Speaker/Persona:** Voice in the poem

### Common Figures of Speech

| Figure | Definition | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| Simile | Comparison using like/as | *He is as brave as a lion.* |
| Metaphor | Direct comparison without like/as | *Life is a journey.* |
| Personification | Giving human traits to non-human | *The wind whispered.* |
| Hyperbole | Exaggeration | *I've told you a million times.* |
| Alliteration | Repeated consonant sounds | *Peter Piper picked...** |
| Onomatopoeia | Words that imitate sound | *Buzz, bang, hiss* |
| Irony | Opposite of what is expected | A fire station catches fire. |
| Euphemism | Mild word for harsh one | *Passed away* = died |

### WAEC Exam Focus
- Answer questions on prescribed texts (drama, prose, poetry).
- Identify themes, characters, literary devices.
